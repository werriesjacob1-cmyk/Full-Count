# F18 source-feasibility decision: individual defender coverage

**Decision: partial individual evidence exists; a receiving-outcome F18 challenger is blocked. No model was fitted and no receiver projection was changed.** This is the requested source gate, not a claim that individual defender performance is unknowable. It protects Claude's F17 and Saturday Tier 1 seal and leaves authoritative B0 untouched.

## What was actually captured

The publicly distributed nflverse [2024 PFR weekly advanced-defense CSV](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/download/pfr_advstats/advstats_week_def_2024.csv.gz) was downloaded directly from its public release for schema and aggregate-coverage inspection; the same asset is referenced by the documented [nflreadr PFR advanced-stat interface](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_pfr_advstats.html). The exact release asset was created 2025-02-13 07:17:35 UTC and updated one second later, before 2025 evaluation games. Captured gzip bytes: 201,021; SHA-256 `55cac555ab518f949bc0ed3e20991f8e51f42ad5278147aae5af347e41e4b4f8`. The raw source is not committed or redistributed here.

The file contains 7,992 player-game rows, 954 distinct PFR defender IDs, 285 games, all 32 teams, and no duplicate `(game_id, pfr_player_id)` keys. Of these, 7,632 rows are regular season and 360 postseason. It directly supplies `def_targets`, `def_completions_allowed`, `def_yards_allowed`, `def_yards_after_catch`, and defender PFR ID/team/opponent. Thus the source does contain **individual target-attributed defensive outcomes**, a stronger fact than on-field participation alone. [PFR's own glossary](https://www.pro-football-reference.com/about/advanced_stats.htm) defines defensive targets and allowed passing outcomes and says its advanced stats come from Sportradar game charting. This is a source-attributed statistic, not an independently verified film label.

There are 2,645 zero/blank-target rows. Another 528 rows have a positive target count but a blank `def_yards_allowed` field. Blank yardage cannot silently become zero, and targets allowed cannot be divided by targets to estimate all-route coverage performance. The file lacks defensive coverage snaps, routes covered, receiver identity, game/play-level assignment, slot/outside alignment, and help/bracket status. A separate [PFR snap-count dataset](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_snap_counts.html) lists defensive snaps, but those are **not coverage-route snaps** and do not by themselves cure the denominator problem.

## Why the receiver consumer stops

The existing [PR #174 tactical adapter](https://github.com/werriesjacob1-cmyk/Full-Count/pull/174) binds targeted receiver identity and play-level *team* coverage labels from nflverse/FTN, while `defense_players` records defenders merely on the field. Its output contains no specific defender responsible for that receiver. [PR #207's F11/F12 report](https://github.com/werriesjacob1-cmyk/Full-Count/pull/207) independently identifies the same absence of receiver–defender assignments and current-season participation. Joining a 2024 defender's allowed yards to a 2025 receiver solely by opposing team would create another team-level proxy, not an individual matchup. The PFR file has defender IDs but no receiving opponent on the attributed target. A player crosswalk from PFR ID to GSIS, if verified, would resolve identity only; it would **not** resolve assignment or expected 2025 involvement. Current injury/replacement and defensive coordinator responsibility are also unverified.

The other public NFL interfaces inspected do not fill that gap. nflreadr's [Next Gen Stats loader](https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_nextgen_stats.html) exposes weekly *passing, receiving, and rushing* stats, not defender coverage assignments. The NFL says its internal [Coverage Responsibility models](https://www.nfl.com/news/next-gen-stats-new-advanced-metrics-you-need-to-know-for-the-2025-nfl-season) can identify defenders' assignments and targeted responsibility, but that description is not a licensed machine-readable feed to this project. Public [NFL Pro](https://www.nfl.com/news/how-to-make-the-most-of-the-nfl-pro-platform-available-through-nfl-premium-subscription) visual statistics are not equivalent to redistribution or model-training rights.

There is a separate **rights blocker**. [Sports Reference's data-use policy](https://www.sports-reference.com/data_use.html) restricts using its site data to support predictive machine-learning systems without permission, and says some provider licenses prohibit redistribution. The underlying advanced-defense charting is [attributed to Sportradar](https://www.pro-football-reference.com/about/advanced_stats.htm). The nflverse repository's public license does not, on its own, prove that Sports Reference or Sportradar granted FULL COUNT the downstream predictive/product rights it needs. This report makes no legal conclusion about every possible use; it records that those rights are **not verified**, so training or publishing a defender-based model on this feed would be premature. No direct PFR scraping or access-control workaround was attempted.

## Gate classification and smallest next step

**Category B for descriptive evidence:** the captured file genuinely measures individual defenders' target-attributed outcomes, with explicit missingness and target-selection limits. **Blocked for the required F18 receiver adjustment:** no defensible expected receiver–defender interaction, all-route denominator, or verified predictive-use rights. Therefore no F18 code, fitted parameters, historical MAE comparison, or invented example appears in this PR. A missing evaluation is the intended fail-closed outcome of this source gate, not a hidden negative accuracy result.

The smallest legitimate route forward is to establish written downstream predictive-use rights for defender coverage data and acquire a feed that links defender ID, receiver ID, game/play, attributed coverage responsibility, coverage opportunity/exposure, and timely availability. It also needs current availability and an evidence-based expected matchup distribution. An NFL/NGS feed might contain relevant coverage-responsibility labels, but [the NFL describes NGS as proprietary data distributed through partners](https://www.nfl.com/_amp/nfl-extends-strategic-partnership-with-genius-sports); access terms and the exact fields must be verified before purchase or integration. Alternatively, permission from the PFR/Sportradar rights chain could enable a narrower defender-level target-conditioned analysis, still without a full receiver matchup. No service has been purchased or contacted.

`source_audit.json` contains the exact asset identity, measured aggregate coverage, and explicit stop decision. The raw charting file, per-defender values, and derivative model features are deliberately absent. No F19 work is started.
